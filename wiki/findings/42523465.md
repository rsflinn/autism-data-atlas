# Clinical, cellular, and genomic consequences of a population-enriched SETD1A missense variant.

## Key Findings
A SETD1A missense variant enriched in a founder population showed allele dose-dependent cognitive effects and near two-fold increased bipolar risk. Homozygous carrier iPSCs showed replication stress, impaired neural rosette formation and early progenitor senescence, with dysregulated modules enriched for autism and psychiatric risk genes. KDM5 inhibition partially rescued the cellular phenotypes.

## Relevance to Atlas
A dose-dependent chromatin-regulator variant with graded phenotype is the kind of intermediate-effect allele this project's threshold model predicts, sitting between de novo loss-of-function and common variation. The founder-population design gives an unusually clean allele-dosage comparison. Weigh cautiously: the primary psychiatric outcome is bipolar disorder, not autism.

## Genes
None of the 49-gene autism set are directly implicated.

## Methods
Exome sequencing n=3,736 Lancaster Old Order Amish, psychiatric and cognitive phenotyping, patient-derived iPSCs, forebrain-directed differentiation, multi-omic profiling, KDM5 inhibitor rescue.
