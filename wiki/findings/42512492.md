# Widespread Hyper-Coupling and Frequency-Specific Dysregulation of Phase-Amplitude Coupling in Young Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder.

## Key Findings
Resting-state EEG in 237 autistic children aged 3-9 versus 201 typically developing controls found stronger coupling between low-frequency phase and gamma amplitude across scalp regions, increased delta-beta coupling, and reduced alpha-beta coupling. Higher coupling strength correlated with LOWER Autism Behavior Checklist scores, meaning stronger coupling tracked with milder measured symptoms.

## Relevance to Atlas
No genomic content. Its value to the atlas is as a severity-phenotype reference point: it is another large dataset in which a physiological disruption metric does not scale with symptom severity in the expected direction, which is the same pattern as the atlas's own severity-disruption disconnect finding. The direction here is inverse, not merely null, and the paper does not establish a mechanism for that.

## Methods
Resting-state EEG, modulation index for phase-amplitude coupling, intra- and inter-region scalp analysis, Bonferroni correction, correlation against Autism Behavior Checklist. Case-control, no genetic data. Data availability not stated in the abstract.
