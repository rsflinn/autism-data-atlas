# Caregiver-reported quality of life among individuals with neurodevelopmental genetic conditions.

## Key Findings
Across 201 individuals with three monogenic neurodevelopmental conditions, caregiver-reported family quality of life tracked with social communication, speech level, challenging behaviors and executive function. Health-related quality of life tracked with sex, mood dysregulation and sleep problems. The two quality-of-life measures had largely non-overlapping predictors.

## Relevance to Atlas
Relevant to the severity-disruption disconnect workstream, which is currently a negative finding across 16 metrics. This paper is a phenotype-side counterpart: it shows which caregiver-reported dimensions separate within single-gene cohorts. PTEN and SYNGAP1 are both in the 49-gene atlas set. It supplies no molecular or regulatory data.

## Genes
PTEN, SYNGAP1

Other genes named in the paper: NFIX

## Methods
Cross-sectional caregiver questionnaire battery, n=201 (43.3% PTEN hamartoma tumor syndrome); two five-step hierarchical regressions.

## Caveats and limits
Caregiver report only, cross-sectional, no molecular data, no comparison to idiopathic autism. Regression correlates are not causal. Cohort is heavily weighted toward PHTS, so SYNGAP1-specific inference is limited. Scored INTEGRATE at 16/50 on phenotype value alone; it contributes nothing to the regulatory-variant side of the atlas.
