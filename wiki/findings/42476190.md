# A cross-scale physical framework for transcription-associated CPEB4 microexon susceptibility and crowding-enhanced isoform self-association: toward a biophysical mechanism of idiopathic autism.

## Key Findings
The authors build a theoretical cross-scale physical model arguing that faster transcription (a proxy for histone-acetylation-associated kinetics) could bias splicing away from CPEB4 microexon 4 (me4), which they find has weaker cis-regulatory support and lower thermodynamic stability than microexon 3. Scaled-particle-theory calculations suggest the resulting delta4-enriched isoforms are more prone to self-association under molecular crowding. This is a modeling and biophysics paper, supported by an orthogonal yeast perturbation system, not measurements in human neurons.

## Relevance to Atlas
Connects to the atlas via CPEB4 microexon mis-splicing, an established idiopathic-autism mechanism (Parras 2018), and to the regulatory-splicing axis rather than to noncoding sequence variants directly. No reusable genomic dataset; no cell-type-resolved or patient phenotype data. Tier C descriptive/theoretical -- no null model or falsification test.

## Genes
None of the 49-gene autism set are the focus. The gene studied, CPEB4, is outside the current set.

## Methods
Simplified transcriptional kinetic model; comparative sequence and structural analysis of CPEB4 microexons; reduced probabilistic splicing model; scaled-particle-theory crowding calculations; yeast perturbation system (butyrate stress) as orthogonal readout.
