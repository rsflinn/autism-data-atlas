# Differentially Methylated Regions in Human Rhombic Lip Compartments Are Enriched in Putative Active Enhancers, Human Accelerated Regions, and Medulloblastoma Copy Number Aberrations.

## Key Findings
Generated DNA methylomes and histone maps from mid-gestation human cerebellar rhombic lip compartments and identified 9,855 differentially methylated regions enriched in active enhancers and human accelerated regions. Twenty-five of these DMRs overlap human accelerated regions near genes implicated in intellectual disability and autism spectrum disorder. The authors infer a gene regulatory network of over 100,000 transcription factor-enhancer-gene links as a framework for interpreting noncoding variation in cerebellar development.

## Relevance to Atlas
Adds a prenatal, cell-type-resolved enhancer and human-accelerated-region map of the developing human cerebellum, useful for interpreting noncoding regulatory variants near autism/ID genes. The >100,000 TF-enhancer-gene links complement the atlas focus on noncoding regulatory architecture, though no members of the 49-gene autism set are named in the abstract.

## Genes
None of the 49-gene autism set are named explicitly in the abstract. Reported transcription-factor drivers of the differentially methylated regions are ATOH1, NEUROD1/2, and HMGA1. ASD-implicated genes near the 25 HAR-overlapping DMRs are not individually named in the abstract.

## Methods
Low-input Enzymatic MethylSeq on microdissected rhombic-lip ventricular zone and subventricular zone (N=9, 15-16 post-conception weeks); histone ChIP profiling of active promoters and enhancers in whole fetal cerebellum (N=6, 14 and 18 weeks); integration with single-cell chromatin accessibility and transcriptomic maps to infer a gene regulatory network. Descriptive epigenomic mapping with enrichment tests (Tier C).
