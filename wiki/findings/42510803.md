# Advances in Functional Genomics for Human Health.

## Key Findings
Review of how functional genomics is used to assign clinical meaning to intergenic and structural abnormalities found by cytogenomic testing when the variant sits outside coding sequence. Cites preaxial polydactyly, Pierre Robin sequence and 5q14.3 microdeletion syndrome as cases where TAD disruption or deletion of a regulatory region, rather than gene-body damage, explains the phenotype. No new data; this is a methods and framing review.

## Relevance to Atlas
Directly on the atlas's central premise: a negative coding test does not rule out a regulatory cause, and the interpretive machinery for intergenic hits already exists in clinical cytogenomics. The 5q14.3 microdeletion example maps to the MEF2C locus by position, though the PubMed abstract record does not preserve the gene symbols the author used -- treat the MEF2C link as [NEEDS VERIFICATION] against full text.

## Genes
MEF2C

## Methods
Narrative review of cytogenomic assays (karyotype, FISH, chromosomal microarray, optical genome mapping) and downstream functional-genomics follow-up including CRISPR-Cas approaches. No primary dataset.
