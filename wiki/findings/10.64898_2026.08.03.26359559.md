# Adults who suspect they may be autistic or have ADHD show corresponding neurodevelopmental polygenic effects

## Key Findings

UK Biobank adults who self-reported suspecting they are autistic or have ADHD without a formal diagnosis were compared with diagnosed and no-diagnosis groups on polygenic scores and co-occurring mental health profiles. GWAS of suspected autism (n=154,926 completed the autism trait questionnaire) and suspected ADHD (n=161,623) were run and genetically correlated with neurodevelopmental and psychiatric conditions.

## Relevance to Atlas

Relevant to the common-variant side of the atlas. The design speaks to ascertainment: if suspected cases carry the same polygenic signal as diagnosed cases, diagnostic status is partly a function of access rather than genetic liability, which affects how case-control GWAS samples should be read. Note the null here matters: the informative comparison is suspected-versus-diagnosed, not suspected-versus-none.

## Genes

None from the 49-gene autism set.

## Methods

UK Biobank self-report classification; GWAS of suspected autism and ADHD; genetic correlation; polygenic score profiling.
