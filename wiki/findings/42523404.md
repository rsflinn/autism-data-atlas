# Age-Related Increases in 40Hz Neural Synchrony Are Specific to Typical Development: A Cross-Sectional Study of Autism Spectrum Disorder.

## Key Findings
Gamma-band phase coherence rose with age in typical development but not in autism without intellectual disability, with divergence appearing only in adulthood. No group differences were detectable under age 18 across TD, ASD with ID and Phelan-McDermid syndrome, and IQ did not predict coherence in clinical groups.

## Relevance to Atlas
A negative finding worth recording: a genetically defined SHANK3 condition was not distinguishable from idiopathic ASD or typical development on an E/I-sensitive measure in childhood. That cuts against expecting monogenic cases to separate cleanly on circuit-level biomarkers, which is relevant to how this project frames genotype-to-phenotype mapping. Cross-sectional design with modest per-group n; age effects are between-subject.

## Genes
SHANK3

## Methods
Cross-sectional 40Hz auditory steady-state response EEG, inter-trial phase coherence, four diagnostic groups, separate models for the two ASD subgroups given differing age and cognitive profiles.
