# Multi-omic data integration improves the resolution of the molecular etiology of autism in a mouse model.

## Key Findings
Integrating RNA-seq, protein-centric 3D chromatin conformation (Hi-ChIP), and whole-genome DNA methylation in hippocampus from Cntnap2 knockout mice narrowed thousands of individually disrupted genes down to 43 ASD-linked genes converging on one pathway: regulation of neuronal synaptic plasticity. Each single omic layer alone flagged broad neuronal disruption (699 downregulated genes; altered chromatin loops; 1220/1659 differentially methylated genes with neuronal function), but only the intersection resolved a specific signal. The authors report consistency with human ASD postmortem expression studies.

## Relevance to Atlas
CNTNAP2 is in the atlas 49-gene set. The paper is a methods proof-of-concept that layering regulatory readouts (chromatin conformation + methylation) on top of expression reduces heterogeneity and points to synaptic-plasticity regulation. This is directionally aligned with the atlas theme that convergence appears at the pathway level while the specific convergent genes are dataset-dependent. Null/base-rate note: this is a single mouse-model study with no case-level replication or specificity test, so it is descriptive (Tier C), not confirmatory.

## Genes
CNTNAP2 (in atlas set). Also reported but outside the 49-gene set: Zbtb18, Cttnbp2, Gabbr2.

## Methods
Bulk hippocampal tissue, Cntnap2 KO vs control mice. RNA-seq, Hi-ChIP (3D chromatin conformation), whole-genome bisulfite sequencing (DNA methylation), with cross-omic integration to a 43-gene intersection followed by GO pathway analysis.

Relates to atlas convergence work (pathway-level convergence real; specific genes vary by dataset).
