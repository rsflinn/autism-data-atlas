# Multi-omic data integration improves the resolution of the molecular etiology of autism in a mouse model.

## Key Findings
Multi-omic integration of RNA-seq, protein-centric 3D chromatin conformation (Hi-ChIP) and whole-genome DNA methylation from Cntnap2 knockout mouse hippocampus. Individual -omics each flagged broad disruption (699 downregulated genes, unique chromatin interactions, 1220/1659 differentially methylated genes with neuronal function); integrating the three layers collapsed the heterogeneity to 43 ASD-linked genes. Those 43 genes converge on a single Gene Ontology term, regulation of neuronal synaptic plasticity, and the authors report consistency with human ASD postmortem expression studies.

## Relevance to Atlas
CNTNAP2 is one of the atlas's 49 autism genes; this is a monogenic-KO model of it. The Hi-ChIP and DNA methylation layers make this a regulatory/epigenetic dataset, not a coding-variant one, so it is a candidate cross-reference for the regulatory-edge and convergence workstreams. Note the convergence is on synaptic plasticity regulation rather than the ion-channel final-common-pathway the atlas has replicated elsewhere; treat the "43 convergent genes" as dataset-specific until tested against a null (prior atlas work found convergent gene identity is dataset-dependent).

## Genes
CNTNAP2. (Other reported hits Zbtb18, Cttnbp2, Gabbr2 are not in the 49-gene set; GABBR2 is a metabotropic GABA-B receptor, distinct from the GABRA/B/G ionotropic subunits in the set.)

## Methods
Bulk hippocampal tissue from Cntnap2 KO vs wild-type mice. RNA-sequencing, HiChIP (protein-centric 3D chromatin conformation), whole-genome bisulfite methylation sequencing, followed by multi-omic data integration and GO pathway analysis. Bulk tissue, not single-cell — no cell-type resolution.
