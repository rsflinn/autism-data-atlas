# Autism-associated substitutions at ASN374 disrupt FOXP2-mediated regulation of DNA binding by TBR1.

## Key Findings
This study reports the first direct biochemical evidence that the FOXP2 forkhead domain binds the TBR1 T-box domain, and that the interaction is tuned by residue Asn374. The autism-associated missense substitutions N374H and N374Y weaken TBR1 binding to T-box DNA and impair FOXP2-dependent regulation: both variants lower FOXP2-TBR1 affinity yet paradoxically resist FOXP2-driven dissociation of TBR1 from DNA. Molecular dynamics simulations place Asn374 as an allosteric control point coupling TBR1 DNA-binding dynamics to FOXP2 engagement.

## Relevance to Atlas
TBR1 and FOXP2 are neurodevelopmental transcription factors in the same regulatory family as FOXP1 (in the atlas 49-gene set). The finding is mechanistic rather than population-level: it shows how specific autism-linked coding variants disrupt a TF-TF regulatory edge controlling DNA binding. This connects to the atlas's interest in regulatory edges between NDD transcription factors, though here the disruption is via protein-coding missense changes, not noncoding variants.

## Genes
None of the 49-gene autism set are named directly. The paper concerns TBR1 and FOXP2, which are functionally adjacent to FOXP1 (a 49-set member) but are not themselves in the set.

## Methods
In vitro biophysics: fluorescence anisotropy and EMSA for binding/affinity; molecular modelling and molecular dynamics simulations for conformational analysis. No genomic dataset deposited.
