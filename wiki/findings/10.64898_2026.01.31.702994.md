# BRD4 represses developmental and neuronal genes through interaction with PRC1.6

## Key Findings

In human embryonic stem cells and neural organoids, BRD4, normally described as a transcriptional co-activator, was found to repress polycomb-regulated developmental and neuronal genes. BRD4 co-occupies bivalent promoters with PRC1.6 and interacts directly with PCGF6 and RING1B through its C-terminal domain; acute BRD4 depletion altered PRC1.6 occupancy.

## Relevance to Atlas

Relevant to the chromatin-regulator end of the gene set. Heterozygous BRD4 loss-of-function causes craniofacial and neurodevelopmental abnormalities, and the mechanism reported here is repression at bivalent promoters rather than the expected activation. That is a reminder that assigning a direction of effect to a chromatin regulator from its general label is unreliable.
