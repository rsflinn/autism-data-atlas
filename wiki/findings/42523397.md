# Developmental signaling reveals functionally enriched human-specific gene regulation in telencephalic progenitors.

## Key Findings
Baseline cross-species expression differences in neural progenitors were extensive but not enriched for developmentally or disease-relevant function. Regulatory divergence that was functionally enriched only appeared under morphogen stimulation, in genes with conserved baseline expression. Those context-dependent response genes were enriched for NDD risk genes and human-lineage sequence changes.

## Relevance to Atlas
The central methodological point maps onto this project's ChromBPNet negative result: regulatory effects that are invisible at baseline can be real under the right developmental context. It is a concrete argument that unstimulated adult-brain models are the wrong assay for developmental regulatory variants, which was a stated limitation of the population burden test.

## Genes
None of the 49-gene autism set are directly implicated.

## Methods
Matched human, chimpanzee and orangutan iPSC-derived telencephalic neuroepithelial cells, morphogen stimulation panel, RNA-seq, enrichment against NDD risk gene sets and human accelerated/human-lineage loci.
