# Arid1b haploinsufficiency in excitatory neurons alters neocortical circuits but not social behavior or seizure phenotypes.

## Key Findings
Restricting Arid1b haploinsufficiency to cortical excitatory neurons in mice altered synaptic connectivity and the excitation/inhibition balance of neocortical circuits, but did not reproduce the social deficits or seizure susceptibility seen in mice with germline (whole-body) Arid1b haploinsufficiency.

## Relevance to Atlas
ARID1B is a high-confidence autism gene (Coffin-Siris syndrome) in the atlas's core gene set. This is a direct test of cell-type-specific contribution to circuit and behavioral phenotypes, relevant to the project's severity-disruption disconnect workstream, which has found that circuit-level disruption metrics don't reliably predict behavioral severity. Null expectation: excitatory-neuron-restricted knockout should reproduce germline phenotypes if excitatory neurons were the primary locus. It did not, which argues against that specific claim rather than for anything positive.

## Genes
ARID1B

## Methods
Transgenic mice, conditional (excitatory-neuron-restricted) versus germline Arid1b haploinsufficiency, paired whole-cell recordings, social behavior assays, seizure susceptibility testing.
