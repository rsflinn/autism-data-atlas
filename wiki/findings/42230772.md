# Estimation of direct and indirect polygenic effects and gene-environment interactions using polygenic scores in case-parent trio studies.

## Key Findings
New method (PGS-TRI) separates direct from parental-indirect polygenic effects in case-parent trios, applied to 18,383 multi-ancestry ASD trios. Estimates transmitted ASD PGS direct effects and finds asymmetrical parental indirect effects (BMI, neurocognitive traits) on child ASD risk. Directly relevant to the atlas's common-variant/polygenic heritability model and the inherited-vs-de-novo distinction.

## Relevance to Atlas
Addresses common inherited (largely noncoding) variant architecture -- ~49% of ASD liability per project model -- and disentangles transmitted vs non-transmitted parental effects, the exact confound that matters for interpreting inherited regulatory burden.

## Genes
None of the 49-gene set are the paper's focus.

## Methods
Polygenic score transmission modeling in trios; simulations under population structure and assortative mating; applied to ASD trios and orofacial cleft trios, plus transcriptome/metabolome-wide PGS.
