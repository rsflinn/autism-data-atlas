# Cell villages and Dirichlet modeling map human cell fitness genetics.

## Key Findings
A pooled 'cell village' screen across 12-39 genetically distinct human neural progenitor lines measured proliferation and survival, with a new Dirichlet-regression tool (Townlet) for the compositional data. NPCs carrying the 16p11.2 deletion, a known autism risk CNV, proliferated faster than controls. Common variants near ZFHX3 associated with NPC proliferation rate, and variants implicating ARNT2 modified sensitivity to lead exposure.

## Relevance to Atlas
This is a common-variant, cell-autonomous readout in a neurodevelopmentally relevant cell type, which is the design gap the ChromBPNet population burden test could not close (adult brain models, no ASD cases). The ZFHX3 proliferation association is a common variant with a measurable cellular phenotype in NPCs. The gene-environment arm (ARNT2 x lead) is a different axis from anything currently in the atlas. No genes from the 49-gene set appear.

## Methods
Pooled multi-donor iPSC-derived NPC 'cell villages' (12-39 lines), demultiplexed by genotype; proliferation/survival measured as changes in line proportions over time; Dirichlet regression (Townlet) for compositional analysis; common-variant association across donors; lead exposure as environmental challenge.

## Caveats and limits
Association-based mapping across a small number of donor lines (12-39). NPC proliferation is a proxy phenotype, not an autism phenotype. No autism cases or phenotype/severity data. Null expectation and replication for the ZFHX3 association are not established from the abstract alone.
