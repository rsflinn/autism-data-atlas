# Context-dependent regulatory variants in Alzheimer's disease

## Key Findings

The preprint addresses how noncoding variants in late-onset Alzheimer's disease perturb regulatory sequence, either by disrupting transcription factor motifs directly or by altering the surrounding regulatory sequence context, and how that affects gene expression and chromatin accessibility.

## Relevance to Atlas

No autism content, logged for method transfer. This is the same class of problem the atlas ChromBPNet work ran into: scoring noncoding variants for regulatory effect and separating real signal from background. The framing of context-dependence, where the same motif disruption matters in one sequence context and not another, is a direct check on the assumptions behind single-variant burden scoring.

## Genes

None from the 49-gene autism set.

## Methods

Regulatory variant effect modeling on chromatin accessibility and expression; TF motif disruption analysis; microglial enhancer annotations.
