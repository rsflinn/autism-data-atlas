# Are CNV Risk Scores Linked to Neurodevelopmental and Mental Health Characteristics Within CNV-Associated Intellectual Disability?

## Key Findings
Within an intellectual disability cohort ascertained on CNV status, summed pLI did not track mental health or neurodevelopmental burden in the expected direction. The relationship reversed above a pLI threshold, with higher-burden CNVs associated with fewer reported difficulties among individuals more likely to have severe ID.

## Relevance to Atlas
Directly relevant to this project's severity-disruption disconnect workstream, which found no relationship between disruption metrics and severity across 16 measures. This is an independent cohort reaching a compatible conclusion by a different route. The reversal is more likely an ascertainment and measurement artifact than a biological effect; the authors flag the confound that severe ID limits DAWBA-style symptom reporting.

## Genes
None of the 49-gene autism set are directly implicated.

## Methods
IMAGINE-ID cohort, clinically reported CNVs annotated with Gencode 19/ENSEMBL, summed pLI and dosage sensitivity scores, multivariate regression on Development and Well-Being Assessment outcomes, stratified post-hoc analysis.
