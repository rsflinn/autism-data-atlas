# Acute rapamycin treatment reveals distinct mechanisms of dysfunction in a maternal inflammation mouse model

## Key Findings
In a mild maternal inflammatory response (MIR) mouse model of ASD, a single acute dose of rapamycin normalized neuronal hyperexcitability, seizure susceptibility, functional network connectivity, repetitive behavior and sensory over-responsivity within two hours. These functional rescues coincided with altered expression of genes associated with ASD, ion channels and epilepsy. The authors argue mTOR dysregulation drives abnormal brain development but the adult brain stays amenable to rapid functional normalization.

## Relevance to Atlas
Reinforces the atlas theme of ion-channel disruption and excitation/inhibition imbalance as a shared downstream pathway in ASD models, and links that pathway to epilepsy comorbidity and to gene-expression (regulatory) dysregulation rather than coding variants. This is a mouse pharmacology study, not a human noncoding-variant analysis, so it is context/mechanism support, not direct evidence for regulatory variants. From the Geschwind group, which also produced the Pasca/Geschwind 2026 idiopathic-ASD work already in the project record.

## Genes
No genes from the 49-gene autism set are named individually in the abstract. The paper refers collectively to "genes associated with ASD, ion channels, and epilepsy" (differential expression), which overlaps the atlas ion-channel set (e.g. SCN2A, SCN1A, KCNB1, CACNA1E) at the category level but not by named locus in the abstract.

## Methods
Maternal inflammatory response mouse model; acute systemic rapamycin (mTOR inhibition); electrophysiology (neuronal excitability, seizure susceptibility); functional network / community-structure connectivity analysis; behavioral assays (repetitive behavior, sensory over-responsivity); differential gene-expression profiling.
