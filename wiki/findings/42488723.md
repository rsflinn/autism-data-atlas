# 16p11.2 microdeletion enhances gene expression variability between human iPSC-derived forebrain interneuron progenitor cells in culture

## Key Findings
Isogenic human iPSC lines differing only by a heterozygous 16p11.2 microdeletion were differentiated into ventral telencephalic (GABAergic) interneuron progenitors and profiled by single-cell RNA-seq. The deletion increased cell-to-cell transcriptional variability genome-wide, for regulon gene sets and for random gene sets alike, indicating the locus normally helps stabilize transcription. Regulons with the largest variability increase were enriched for cell-cycle genes and were controlled by transcription factors linked to autism or obesity.

## Relevance to Atlas
Cell-type-resolved regulatory evidence that a top autism CNV destabilizes gene-regulatory networks in GABAergic interneuron progenitors. Speaks to the atlas themes of regulatory-network dysregulation and interneuron involvement and provides a reusable single-cell dataset. None of the 49 atlas genes sit inside 16p11.2, so the link is thematic (regulatory variability, interneuron development) rather than gene-level.

## Genes
None of the 49-gene set. The variant is a deletion of the 16p11.2 locus (genes such as KCTD13, TAOK2, MAPK3), which is not represented in the atlas gene set.

## Methods
Isogenic iPSC differentiation to ventral telencephalic interneuron progenitors; single-cell RNA-seq; regulon and transcriptional-variability bioinformatics.
