# GABAergic neuron fate specification and lineage allocation: from development to disorder.

## Key Findings
This review describes how cis-regulatory elements (CREs) and the transcription factors that bind them form gene regulatory networks that establish the molecular identity of telencephalic inhibitory (GABAergic) neuron subtypes. Many variants associated with neurological and mental disorders affect proteins acting at CREs or alter their regulatory activity. The authors argue that mapping risk-gene expression onto adult cell taxonomies often fails to explain circuit dysfunction, and synthesize evidence for a model in which genetic variation modulates developmental timing, fate bias and lineage allocation, producing imbalanced interneuron subtype proportions among otherwise normal cell types.

## Relevance to Atlas
Directly relevant to the atlas's regulatory and cell-type focus. It frames disorder risk as acting through noncoding cis-regulatory elements during development rather than through adult steady-state expression, which matches the atlas's working interest in regulatory variants and developmental-window effects (cf. fetal-brain eQTL findings where signals reverse or disappear postnatally). GABAergic interneuron balance is also the circuit substrate connecting autism and epilepsy. As a review it deposits no data and makes no statistical claim, so it is a framing/context resource, not evidence.
