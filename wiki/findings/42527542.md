# Fever-like temperatures improve cognitive deficits in the Scn2a autism mouse model via increases in potassium channel activity.

## Key Findings
Fever-range temperature, separated from immune activation, restored learning and novelty recognition in Scn2a mutant mice. The mechanism was increased potassium channel activation raising spike threshold and reducing excess pyramidal-neuron spiking in somatosensory cortex. Chemogenetic spiking reduction at normal temperature reproduced the rescue.

## Relevance to Atlas
Sodium-channel loss producing a phenotype correctable by shifting potassium channel activity is a direct test of the ion-channel-balance framing this project has tracked. It supports excitability as the proximate lever rather than the gene identity itself. Null expectation would have been no behavioral change with temperature; the chemogenetic replication is the useful control.
